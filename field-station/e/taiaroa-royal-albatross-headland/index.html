About this place

Analyze Diomedea sanfordi shoulder-lock biomechanics, Rayleigh-cycle dynamic soaring, and Earth's only mainland albatross colony.

For kids: Stand on a windy New Zealand cliff where giant ocean gliders lock three-metre wings open and circle the whole planet without flapping!

Taiaroa Head Royal Albatrosses · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Tussock nest mound ('toroa' nursery) — Diomedea sanfordi biennial breeding. Centre the silver-tussock slope hosting volcanic-soil nest pedestals
  • Roaring-Forties launch bluff — Shoulder-lock tendon & runway lift. Locate the windward headland lip providing high-lift takeoff airflow
  • Southern Ocean dynamic-soaring waves — Boundary-layer wind-gradient extraction. Scan the open Southern Ocean horizon where boundary-layer wind shear powers flight

Source: Sachs et al. (2012), PLOS ONE & Meyers & Stakebake (2005), Journal of Morphology — Dynamic soaring S-trajectories & procellariiform shoulder-lock anatomy.

Ask together: Hold both your arms straight out to the sides for sixty seconds—feel your shoulders tire, and imagine how handy an albatross's 'wing-latch' tendon is!

Field quiz

Kids' question: How can a royal albatross glide thousands of miles across windy oceans without getting sore wing muscles?

  • A built-in shoulder tendon latch locks its wings open while it surfs fast and slow wind layers
  • Friendly dolphins push it from underneath
  • It lands on passing ships every ten minutes
Answer

A built-in shoulder tendon latch locks its wings open while it surfs fast and slow wind layers

Adults' question: Where does the kinetic energy come from that allows Diomedea albatrosses to fly upwind and crosswind for days without flapping (Sachs et al., 2012)?

  • Vertical wind-speed shear in the lowest 15–20 m above ocean waves harvested via cyclic S-turns
  • Hot volcanic thermals erupting continuously from abyssal trenches
  • Solar-heated air sacs inflating inside hollow wing quills
  • Electrostatic repulsion from salty ocean spray
Answer

Vertical wind-speed shear in the lowest 15–20 m above ocean waves harvested via cyclic S-turns
