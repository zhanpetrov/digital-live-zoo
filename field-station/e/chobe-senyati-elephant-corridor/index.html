About this place

Explore the KAZA transfrontier corridor, matriarchal drought-memory repositories (McComb et al., 2001), and trunk hydrostats.

For kids: Follow wise grandmother elephants guiding giant families along ancient memory trails where four countries meet!

Chobe Four-Country Elephant Way · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Ancient Kalahari elephant trail — KAZA transboundary movement corridor. Locate the ancestral Kalahari sand highway linking Botswana, Zimbabwe, Zambia, and Namibia
  • Dry-season pan & trunk drinking lip — Muscular hydrostat nasal dilation. Centre the waterhole margin where elephants draw up to 9 litres per trunk cycle
  • Zambezi teak & mopane woodland — Baikiaea plurijuga ecosystem engineering. Scan the Baikiaea-Colophospermum woodland shaped by megaherbivore canopy opening

Source: McComb et al. (2001), Science & Schulz et al. (2021), Journal of the Royal Society Interface — Matriarchal social/ecological knowledge & elephant trunk suction mechanics.

Kids' question: How many bones are hidden inside an elephant's super-bendy, heavy-lifting trunk?

  • Zero bones—it's pure interwoven muscle like your tongue!
  • Exactly one hundred tiny rib bones
  • One long stiff broomstick bone
Answer

Zero bones—it's pure interwoven muscle like your tongue!

Adults' question: What key advantage did McComb et al. (2001, Science) demonstrate in elephant families led by older matriarchs?

  • Superior discrimination of unfamiliar social calls and multi-decade spatial memory of drought refugia
  • Faster sprinting speeds above 70 km/h across open pans
  • Ability to photosynthesize sugars through large ear pinnae
  • Complete independence from liquid drinking water for six months
Answer

Superior discrimination of unfamiliar social calls and multi-decade spatial memory of drought refugia
