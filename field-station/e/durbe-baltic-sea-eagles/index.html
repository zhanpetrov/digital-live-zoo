About this place

Analyze Haliaeetus albicilla apex nesting above Baltic raised Sphagnum peatlands and millennia-scale carbon sequestration.

For kids: Soar to the top of a giant Latvian spruce tree where barn-door-winged sea eagles watch over ancient mossy bogs!

Durbe Baltic Sea-Eagle Crown · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Emergent spruce crown eyrie — Haliaeetus albicilla perennial platform. Locate the wind-exposed emergent conifer crown supporting an apex eagle eyrie
  • Raised Sphagnum peat dome — Ombrotrophic Sphagnum carbon sink. Centre the ombrotrophic raised bog dome where Sphagnum acidifies porewater
  • Shallow pike & coot lake — Esox lucius & Fulica atra prey zone. Scan the shallow eutrophic lake margin supplying pike, bream, and waterbirds

Source: Helander et al. (2008) & Clymo (1984), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B — Baltic Haliaeetus albicilla recovery from organochlorines and Sphagnum peat carbon accumulation.

Field quiz

Kids' question: Why doesn't dead plant stuff rot away quickly inside a wet Latvian Sphagnum bog?

  • The moss makes water sour like lemon juice and leaves almost zero air for rot-bugs
  • Tiny bog fairies vacuum all leaves every Friday
  • Boiling underground lava toasts the moss dry
Answer

The moss makes water sour like lemon juice and leaves almost zero air for rot-bugs

Adults' question: What biochemical mechanism makes ombrotrophic Sphagnum bogs among Earth's densest terrestrial carbon sinks?

  • Cation exchange releasing H+ ions, phenolic sphagnan compounds, and waterlogged anoxia inhibiting phenol oxidase
  • Rapid calcification by marine coral polyps inside peat pores
  • High-velocity oxygen bubbling from deep limestone geysers
  • Complete sterilization by ultraviolet glare off snow year-round
Answer

Cation exchange releasing H+ ions, phenolic sphagnan compounds, and waterlogged anoxia inhibiting phenol oxidase
