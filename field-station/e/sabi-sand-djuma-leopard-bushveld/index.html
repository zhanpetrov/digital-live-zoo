About this place

Examine Panthera pardus arboreal caching against hyena kleptoparasitism, heterospecific alarm networks, and scotopic vision.

For kids: Explore shady marula trees where spotted leopards nap on high branches and noisy alarm birds tattle from above!

Sabi Sand Tree-Climbing Leopards · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • High marula tree branch fork — Sclerocarya birrea anti-kleptoparasite cache. Scan the marula crown forks used by leopards to hoist prey above terrestrial scavengers
  • Sun-dappled bushveld grass & scrub — Disruptive rosette camouflage & tapetum. Centre the dappled Lowveld thicket matched by disruptive Panthera pardus rosettes
  • Grey go-away-bird treetop perch — Crinifer concolor heterospecific eavesdropping. Locate the exposed snag perch used by grey go-away-birds and vervet sentinels

Source: Balme et al. (2017), Journal of Animal Ecology — Arboreal caching & kleptoparasitism evasion by Panthera pardus in Sabi Sand.

Field quiz

Kids' question: Why do Sabi Sand leopards work so hard to carry their food high up into forked marula trees?

  • To keep their meal safe out of reach of ground-walking hyenas and lions
  • Because tree squirrels help season it with acorns
  • To dry it in the wind like laundry
Answer

To keep their meal safe out of reach of ground-walking hyenas and lions

Adults' question: Which anatomical structure gives nocturnal leopards six-to-seven-fold higher light sensitivity than diurnal primates?

  • A reflective riboflavin/zinc tapetum cellulosum behind a rod-dominated retina
  • Compound multifaceted corneas split into hexagonal ommatidia
  • Bioluminescent tear ducts illuminating prey trails
  • Infrared thermoreceptor pits lining the upper whiskers
Answer

A reflective riboflavin/zinc tapetum cellulosum behind a rod-dominated retina
