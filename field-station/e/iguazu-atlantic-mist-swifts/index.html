What to look for

  • Roaring waterfall swift doorway — Cypseloides senex hydro-barrier entry. Centre the sheer cataract sheet penetrated by commuting Cypseloides senex
  • Spray-shielded basalt cliff ledges — Serra Geral basalt & mucronate tail prop. Locate the wet flood-basalt steps where swifts cling via needle claws and tail spines
  • Rainbow mist & aerial insect plume — Cataract updraft & Podostemaceae riverbeds. Scan the turbulent spray plume lifting aquatic insects into swift foraging lanes

Source: Marín (2000) & Macchi et al. (2021), Ornithology Research — Cypseloides senex cataract-penetration flight and vertical wet-cliff nesting.

Adults' question: Which anatomical pair allows Cypseloides senex to roost vertically for hours on slick, spray-soaked basalt walls behind waterfalls?

  • Needle-sharp curved claws paired with stiffened, spine-tipped tail feathers acting as a vertical brace
  • Suction-cup toe pads identical to glass tree frogs
  • Webbed diving paddles and solid marrowless leg bones
  • Prehensile hooked bills clamped into basalt cracks
