What to look for

  • Terpene-hazed eucalypt canyon — Volatile cineole/pinene Rayleigh scatter. Centre the distant gorge veiled in volatile eucalypt terpene aerosols
  • Ribbon-gum koala sleeping fork — Phascolarctos cinereus CYP2C & trunk hugging. Locate the smooth-barked Eucalyptus viminalis fork favored by folivorous koalas
  • Sandstone slab & wombat cube cairn — Vombatus ursinus two-stiff-band colon. Scan the elevated rock perch where bare-nosed wombats deposit cubic territorial scats

Source: Johnson et al. (2018), Nature Genetics — Koala CYP2C gene expansion; Yang et al. (2021), Soft Matter — Intestinal non-uniform stiffness forms cubic wombat feces.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How do Australian wombats make cube-shaped droppings that stay put on top of round rocks without rolling off?

  • Two stiff bands and two stretchy bands inside their intestine gently press grass into four corners!
  • They pat them flat afterwards with tiny wooden trowels
  • They eat square sandstone pebbles
Answer

Two stiff bands and two stretchy bands inside their intestine gently press grass into four corners!

Adults' question: What genomic discovery explained how koalas taste and safely metabolize toxic terpene-rich Eucalyptus foliage (Johnson et al., 2018)?

  • Massive tandem expansion of cytochrome P450 (CYP2C) liver detox genes paired with amplified bitter-taste and aquaporin receptors
  • Complete absence of liver tissue compensated by gill excretion
  • Converting cineole directly into diamond crystals in the spleen
  • Outsourcing all digestion to external fungus gardens in burrows
Answer

Massive tandem expansion of cytochrome P450 (CYP2C) liver detox genes paired with amplified bitter-taste and aquaporin receptors
