About this place

Analyze cold Benguela upwelling along hyper-arid Namib dunes, Arctocephalus pusillus rookeries, and Stenocara fog harvesting.

For kids: Explore where golden desert sand dunes touch icy ocean waves—home to barking fur seals, pink flamingos, and fog-drinking beetles!

Skeleton Coast Fog, Dunes & Seals · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Pelican Point Cape fur seal beach — Arctocephalus pusillus Benguela rookery. Centre the Pelican Point sandspit supporting tens of thousands of Cape fur seals
  • Fog-kissed golden Namib dunes — Stenocara gracilipes elytral fog harvesting. Locate the coastal dune slipfaces where advective marine fog sustains endemic arthropods
  • Protected pink flamingo lagoon — Lamellar filter-feeding & carotenoid pigments. Scan the sheltered Ramsar tidal flats partitioning greater and lesser flamingo foraging

Source: Parker & Lawrence (2001), Nature & Shannon (1985), Oceanography and Marine Biology — Stenocara hydrophilic/hydrophobic fog capture & Benguela upwelling.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How does a tiny Namib Desert beetle get a fresh morning drink where rain almost never falls?

  • It tilts into sea fog so bumpy wing backs catch water drops that roll down to its mouth
  • It orders coconut milk from passing pelicans
  • It digs a tunnel ten miles down to magma
Answer

It tilts into sea fog so bumpy wing backs catch water drops that roll down to its mouth

Adults' question: Which dual surface microstructure on Stenocara gracilipes elytra inspired biomimetic atmospheric water harvesters (Parker & Lawrence, 2001)?

  • Alternating hydrophilic non-waxy peaks for droplet nucleation and hydrophobic waxy troughs for rapid runoff
  • Electrically heated metallic scales boiling seawater spray
  • Porous calcareous shells absorbing liquid brine through the feet
  • Suction-pumping deep groundwater via a two-metre taproot
Answer

Alternating hydrophilic non-waxy peaks for droplet nucleation and hydrophobic waxy troughs for rapid runoff
