About this place

Examine spring-fed Jutland Fagus sylvatica streams shared by underwater-foraging Cinclus cinclus, kingfishers, and roe deer.

For kids: Peer along a crystal-clear Danish woodland stream where plump little songbirds walk right underneath rushing water!

Danish Beech-Stream Dippers & Kingfishers · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Mossy boulder dipper riffle — Cinclus cinclus benthic foraging riffle. Locate the oxygenated cobble riffle where dippers probe for Trichoptera larvae
  • Overhanging kingfisher snag — Alcedo atthis structural photonic blue. Centre the streamside root perch overlooking a minnow pool used by Alcedo atthis
  • Unfreezing spring-fed deer ford — Constant-temperature groundwater seep. Scan the groundwater-seep crossing maintaining ice-free winter flow

Source: Ormerod & Tyler (2005) & Goodge (1960), Auk — Cinclus cinclus aquatic passerine physiology, nictitating lens accommodation, and Ephemeroptera/Trichoptera bioindication.

Ask together: Most birds float like corks on water! How do you think a tiny round songbird manages to stay down on the bottom of a fast stream?

Adults' question: Why is the presence of breeding Cinclus cinclus considered a gold-standard bioindicator of stream water quality?

  • It drinks ten litres of chlorinated water every afternoon
  • Its feathers dissolve instantly in slightly cool rainwater
  • It eats exclusively invasive plastic-digesting algae
