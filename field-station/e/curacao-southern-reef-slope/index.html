About this place

Study hurricane-sheltered Southern Caribbean fringing slopes, cryptochrome-cued broadcast spawning, and Chromis planktivore walls.

For kids: Dive south of the hurricane belt where shimmering blue fish catch drifting snacks and full moons trigger pink coral snowstorms!

Curaçao Lunar-Spawning Reef Slope · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Blue chromis plankton wall — Chromis cyanea pelagic-benthic subsidy. Centre the shelf-break water column where Chromis shoals intercept pelagic copepods
  • Mountainous star coral domes — Orbicella faveolata cryptochrome spawning. Locate the multi-century Orbicella colonies famous for synchronized lunar spawning
  • Giant barrel sponge water pumps — Xestospongia muta sponge loop. Scan the lower reef slope colonized by Xestospongia muta driving the 'sponge loop'

Source: Levy et al. (2007), Science & Vermeij et al. (2010), PLOS ONE — Coral cryptochrome lunar photoreception and Curaçao broadcast spawning.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How do corals with no eyes know the exact night after a full moon to release their floating pink egg bundles?

  • Blue-light proteins in their skin sense gentle moonlight above the reef!
  • Passing sea turtles tap on every coral dome
  • The ocean turns hot like soup on full moons
Answer

Blue-light proteins in their skin sense gentle moonlight above the reef!

Adults' question: What is the 'sponge loop' discovered on Curaçao's coral reefs by de Goeij et al. (2013, Science)?

  • Encrusting and barrel sponges absorb dissolved organic matter from corals and shed detritus consumed by reef fauna
  • Sponges photosynthesize oxygen faster than tropical kelps
  • Barrel sponges trap whole schools of blue tangs inside oscula
  • Sponges dissolve limestone cliffs to release atmospheric nitrogen
Answer

Encrusting and barrel sponges absorb dissolved organic matter from corals and shed detritus consumed by reef fauna
