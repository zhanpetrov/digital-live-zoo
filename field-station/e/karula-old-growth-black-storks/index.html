About this place

Explore shy forest-interior Ciconia nigra nesting ecology and ectomycorrhizal carbon-sharing networks beneath Baltic pines.

For kids: Tiptoe into quiet Estonian pine woods where shy glossy-feathered black storks nest above secret fungal tree networks!

Karula Old-Growth Black Storks · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Ancient lateral nest pine — Ciconia nigra sub-canopy platform. Locate the mature lower-canopy bough architecture required for massive black stork stick nests
  • Mossy mycorrhizal forest floor — Ectomycorrhizal hyphal network. Centre the undisturbed humus floor housing ectomycorrhizal root symbioses
  • Shaded woodland brook — Brook lamprey & loach foraging stream. Scan the clean canopy-shaded stream corridor where adult storks stalk brook fish and amphibians

Source: Simard et al. (1997), Nature & Lõhmus et al. (2005), Biological Conservation — Bidirectional mycorrhizal carbon transfer and old-growth habitat selection by Ciconia nigra.
