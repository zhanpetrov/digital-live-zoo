About this place

Examine Gorilla beringei graueri knuckle-walking wrist morphology, hindgut cellulolysis, and nightly nest engineering.

For kids: Meet gentle giant gorillas who walk on curled knuckles and weave a brand-new leafy bed every single night!

Eastern Congo Grauer's Gorillas · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Wild ginger & Marantaceae stems — Aframomum & hindgut fiber fermentation. Locate dense Aframomum and Marantaceae herbs fermented in the gorilla's enlarged colon
  • Springy woven night nest — Arboreal/terrestrial thermoregulatory bed. Centre the woven foliage zone used for single-night insulated sleeping platforms
  • Fruiting rainforest canopy — Endozoochorous megafaunal seed dispersal. Scan the upper fruiting crowns dependent on large-ape gut seed dispersal

Source: Kivell & Schmitt (2009), PNAS & Stewart, Pruetz & Hansell (2007) — Carpal biomechanics of knuckle-walking & great-ape nest construction.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How often does a grown-up Grauer's gorilla weave itself a fresh leafy sleeping nest?

  • Every single evening before going to sleep
  • Only once when it turns ten years old
  • Never—gorillas sleep hanging upside-down by their toes
Answer

Every single evening before going to sleep

Adults' question: Which anatomical adaptation protects a Grauer's gorilla's wrist joints during heavy terrestrial knuckle-walking?

  • Extended dorsal metacarpal ridges and tightly interlocking carpal bones resisting hyperextension
  • Fluid-filled pneumatic air sacs inside hollow finger bones
  • Fused hoof-like keratin sheaths covering all five digits
  • Cartilaginous suction pads beneath the palms
Answer

Extended dorsal metacarpal ridges and tightly interlocking carpal bones resisting hyperextension
