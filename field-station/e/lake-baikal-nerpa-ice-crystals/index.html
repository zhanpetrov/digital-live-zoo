About this place

Explore the Baikal continental rift abyss (20% of global liquid surface freshwater), Epischura baikalensis biofiltration, and endemic Pusa sibirica.

For kids: Peer through glass-clear Siberian ice into Earth's deepest lake—kept sparkling clean by tiny shrimp helpers and home to round freshwater seals!

Lake Baikal Crystal Ice & Nerpa Seals · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Glass-clear methane-bubble & turquoise ice — Epischura baikalensis ultra-oligotrophy. Centre the ultra-transparent fast ice formed from low-turbidity rift water
  • Turquoise pressure-ridge snow lair — Pusa sibirica produshina & birth lair. Locate the hummocked pressure ridge sheltering subnivean nerpa pupping lairs
  • Ice-festooned Olkhon rift cliffs — 1,642 m continental rift graben. Scan the fault-scarp headland bounding Earth's deepest, oldest lake basin

Source: Palo & Väinölä (2006), Journal of Evolutionary Biology — Pleistocene Arctic origins of Pusa sibirica; Moore et al. (2009), BioScience — Lake Baikal endemic food web.

Ask together: Lake Baikal sits thousands of miles inland from any salty ocean—how do you think seal ancestors ever traveled all the way to the middle of Siberia during the Ice Age?
