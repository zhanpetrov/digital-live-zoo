About this place

Investigate drowned ria geomorphology, forest-floor kororā nesting ecology, and seabird-driven marine-to-terrestrial nutrient subsidies.

For kids: Explore emerald sunken river valleys where little penguins cozy up right under giant silver tree-ferns in the forest!

Marlborough Sounds Forest Penguins · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Silver-fern & root-hollow penguin nest — Alsophila dealbata forest-floor burrow. Centre the coastal broadleaf-fern understory where kororā excavate root burrows
  • Sheltered emerald ria inlet — Tectonic subsidence & post-glacial ria. Locate the sheltered ria channel where Cook Strait currents feed baitfish schools
  • Guano-enriched beech & bellbird canopy — Marine δ15N soil-to-canopy trophic cascade. Scan the coastal forest canopy fertilized by burrowing seabird marine nutrients

Source: Fukami et al. (2006), Ecology Letters & Mulder et al. (2011) — Seabird-driven marine nutrient subsidies shaping New Zealand coastal forest ecosystems.

Ask together: Most people picture penguins standing on snowy white ice—why do New Zealand's forest penguins love nesting under shady green tree-ferns instead?

Adults' question: How do burrowing penguins and shearwaters reshape coastal forest food webs across New Zealand sanctuary islands (Fukami et al., 2006)?

  • Importing marine nitrogen/phosphorus via guano and aerating soil through burrowing, multiplying litter fauna and canopy productivity
  • Stripping all bark off southern beech trees to weave floating rafts
  • Salting the topsoil until only desert cacti survive
  • Consuming all forest earthworms during winter hibernation
