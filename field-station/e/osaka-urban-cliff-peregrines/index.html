What to look for

  • Wind-sheltered high-rise gravel ledge — Anthropogenic cliff-ledge scrape. Centre the recessed high-rise ledge functioning as a cliff scrape
  • Thermal updraft stoop ceiling — Nasal bony tubercle pressure baffle. Locate the high thermal column launching >320 km/h hunting stoops
  • Osaka Bay sea-breeze canyon — Orographic building-deflection lift. Scan the bay-front facade generating vertical deflection updrafts

Source: Ponitz et al. (2014), PLOS ONE & Gowree et al. (2018), Communications Biology — Aerodynamics, vortex lift, and nasal baffle pressure regulation in diving Falco peregrinus.

Field quiz

Kids' question: What special feature inside a peregrine falcon's nostrils lets it breathe during a super-fast dive?

  • Tiny cone baffles that slow down rushing wind—just like jet engine intakes
  • Watertight rubber plugs that shut for an hour
  • Miniature spinning propellers on its beak
Answer

Tiny cone baffles that slow down rushing wind—just like jet engine intakes

Adults' question: How do bony nasal tubercles and M-wing cup feathers work together during a Falco peregrinus high-speed stoop?

  • Nasal tubercles decelerate intake ram pressure while popped covert feathers prevent boundary-layer separation
  • Nostrils seal shut while oxygen is absorbed directly through hollow wing bones
  • Sub-dermal air bladders inflate to slow the bird like a parachute
  • Echolocation clicks part air molecules ahead of the cere
