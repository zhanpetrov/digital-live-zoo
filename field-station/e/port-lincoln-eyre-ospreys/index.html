About this place

Examine Pandion cristatus reversible zygodactyl grip, head-first drag reduction, and Spencer Gulf Posidonia blue-carbon meadows.

For kids: Watch sharp-eyed ospreys flip slippery fish nose-first like tiny torpedoes above glowing blue seagrass meadows!

Port Lincoln Seagrass & Ospreys · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Coastal lookout & stick platform — Pandion cristatus zygodactyl spicules. Centre the elevated coastal platform used by Pandion cristatus pairs
  • Emerald ribbon-weed seagrass meadow — Posidonia australis nursery & blue carbon. Locate the subtidal Posidonia seagrass beds sheltering juvenile teleosts
  • Spencer Gulf inverse-estuary channel — Seasonal temperature-salinity front. Scan the mouth of Boston Bay opening toward Spencer Gulf's productive shelf

Source: Poole (1989) & Sustaita et al. (2013), Journal of Experimental Biology — Pandion reversible zygodactyly & spicules; Lavery et al. (2013), PLOS ONE — Australian Posidonia blue carbon.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How does an osprey carry a freshly caught fish in its claws as it flies back to its nest?

  • Pointed straight nose-first like a torpedo so wind slips smoothly past!
  • Sideways like a giant handlebar
  • Balanced on top of its head like a hat
Answer

Pointed straight nose-first like a torpedo so wind slips smoothly past!

Adults' question: Why is the eastern osprey (Pandion cristatus) grouped in its own family Pandionidae separate from Accipitridae eagles and hawks?

  • Reversible outer fourth toe, spiny plantar spicules, closable nasal valves, and dense oily plumage for full-submersion dives
  • Complete absence of talons replaced by webbed duck feet
  • Echolocation sonar clicks emitted underwater
  • Gills hidden beneath ear coverts for five-minute dives
Answer

Reversible outer fourth toe, spiny plantar spicules, closable nasal valves, and dense oily plumage for full-submersion dives
