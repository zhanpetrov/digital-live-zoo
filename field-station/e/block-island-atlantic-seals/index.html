About this place

Examine Halichoerus grypus undulating vibrissae Kármán-vortex suppression and hydrodynamic wake tracking.

For kids: Visit a windy Atlantic sandbar where seals curl into 'banana poses' and track fish wakes with wavy whiskers!

Block Island Whisker-Radar Seals · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Sandy Point haul-out sandbar — Halichoerus grypus & Phoca vitulina haul-out. Centre the undisturbed terminal sand spit used for thermal haul-out
  • Two-current hydrodynamic rip — Undulating vibrissae Kármán-vortex trail. Locate the turbulent tidal convergence where seals track sand-lance wakes
  • Dune grass & North Light bluff — Ammophila breviligulata barrier dunes. Scan the marram-grass barrier dunes stabilizing Block Island's northern tip

Source: Dehnhardt et al. (2001), Science; Hanke et al. (2010), Journal of Experimental Biology — Pinniped vibrissal wake tracking & vortex suppression.

Kids' question: Why do harbor and gray seals have bumpy, wavy whiskers instead of smooth round ones like a housecat?

  • Wavy bumps keep whiskers steady in currents so they can feel a swimming fish's trail!
  • To curl fancy hairstyles on the beach
  • To catch falling snowflakes

Adults' question: What biomimetic engineering principle was discovered in the undulating geometry of phocid seal vibrissae (Hanke et al., 2010)?

  • Suppression of self-induced Kármán vortex-induced vibrations, enabling detection of prey wakes up to 35 seconds later
  • Generation of ultrasonic sonar clicks from whisker tips
  • Bioluminescent attraction of Atlantic squid
  • Filtering microscopic phytoplankton like baleen plates
