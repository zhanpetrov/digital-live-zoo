About this place

Sunrise Watch: First Light at the Pan

Chyulu Hills waterhole hide, 06:08 EAT sunrise watch.

What to look for

  • African bush elephant family — Loxodonta africana. Log the Loxodonta africana matriarch, allomother & calf at the bank
  • Maasai giraffes at first light — Giraffa tippelskirchi. Log the two Maasai giraffes (Giraffa tippelskirchi) along the acacia line
  • Equatorial sunrise (06:08 EAT) — Partly cloudy dawn · ~17°C. Log the eastern sunrise glow & partly-cloudy dawn cumulus layer
  • Dawn waterhole mist — Radiative night cooling over warm pan water. Log the thermal evaporation mist across the waterhole surface

Source: McComb et al. (2001), Science & Moss (1988) — Matriarchal leadership and anti-predator calf-flanking in Loxodonta africana.

Ask together: Notice where the tiny elephant calf walks! Who in your family walks on the outside when you cross a busy street together?

Savanna Breeze: Mud Baths & Canopy Browsers

Mid-morning watch (~24°C, partly cloudy fair-weather cumulus, 17 km/h ESE breeze).

What to look for

  • Elephant mud & water shower — Evaporative cooling & UV barrier. Log the trunk-spraying thermoregulation behaviour in the pan
  • Cattle egrets — Bubulcus ibis. Log the commensal cattle egrets (Bubulcus ibis) on the shoreline
  • Umbrella thorn browsing — Vachellia tortilis · upper-canopy guild. Log canopy-stratified giraffe browsing on Vachellia tortilis
  • Plains zebras — Equus quagga. Log the peripheral Plains zebra herd (Equus quagga)

Source: Lillywhite & Stein (1987) & Martins et al. (2018), Nature Communications — Elephant skin micro-channel water retention and savanna browser niche partitioning.

Ask together: If giraffes eat top leaves, zebras munch low grass, and egrets catch bugs, how is a savanna waterhole like a polite family dinner table?

Golden Hour to Sunset Dusk (Full 5:51 Camp)

Sunset dusk watch (18:19 EAT). Convective wind drops and crepuscular transition begins. Log the three dusk bearings, or use the timeline chapters to compare all four watches across the full 5:51 stream.

What to look for

  • Savanna sunset (18:19 EAT) — Crepuscular transition · wind drop. Log the western solar azimuth (~092° grid / Copper dusk horizon)
  • Last drink before dark — Evening hydration & bush withdrawal. Log the dusk matriarch & calf drinking before withdrawing to bush cover
  • Giraffes in sunset rim-light — Nocturnal open-plain positioning. Log the rim-lit giraffe pair moving toward open night-roost grassland

Source: Gaynor et al. (2018), Science & Garstang et al. (1995) — Crepuscular niche shifts and nocturnal boundary-layer infrasound propagation.

Ask together: Giraffes take tiny standing micro-naps at night! What helps you feel safe and cozy before going to sleep?

See it live: Live check: Elephant Plain, ol Donyo

By day
African bush elephants, Maasai giraffes, Plains zebras & fringe-eared oryx Ol Donyo's open waterhole faces Mount Kilimanjaro across the Chyulu–Amboseli corridor. Daylight brings matriarchal elephant herds, giraffes and plains game; wind and cloud cover come live from MET Norway.
By night
Bull elephants, spotted hyenas, lions or large-spotted genets on the infrared feed After 18:19 EAT sunset, ol Donyo switches to low-light/infrared imaging: solitary bull elephants frequently drink after dark alongside spotted hyenas and prides moving along the waterhole track.
