About this place

Unpack the Bradypus–Cryptoses–Trichophilus three-way mutualism (Pauli et al., 2014) and inverted ribcage adhesion anatomy.

For kids: Hang upside down with smiling three-toed sloths wearing a living green garden coat of friendly moths and algae!

Costa Rica Sloth-Garden Canopy · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Cecropia upside-down hammock fork — Bradypus fibrinous visceral adhesions. Centre the open Cecropia crown where sloths thermoregulate in inverted suspension
  • Moss-green fur camouflage canopy — Trichophilus welckeri pelage symbiosis. Locate the dappled mid-canopy foliage matching Trichophilus-tinted sloth pelage
  • Weekly tree-base moth nursery — Cryptoses choloepi oviposition site. Scan the buttress-root base visited weekly to sustain mutualistic Cryptoses moths

Source: Pauli et al. (2014), Proc. R. Soc. B & Cliffe et al. (2014), Biology Letters — Sloth-moth-algae mutualism and fibrinous organ adhesions.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How can a three-toed sloth hang upside down for hours without its heavy stomach squishing its lungs?

  • Built-in stretchy ribbons hold its stomach neatly to its ribs like a seatbelt!
  • Sloths breathe through tiny holes in their claws
  • Their stomachs float because they eat helium bubbles
Answer

Built-in stretchy ribbons hold its stomach neatly to its ribs like a seatbelt!

Adults' question: Why do three-toed sloths make an energetically costly weekly descent to the forest floor instead of defecating from the canopy (Pauli et al., 2014)?

  • To deliver Cryptoses moths to fresh dung so future moth generations fertilize digestible Trichophilus fur algae
  • To hunt terrestrial leaf-cutter ant queens for protein
  • To sharpen curved claws against volcanic river pebbles
  • To cool overheated muscles in subterranean burrows
Answer

To deliver Cryptoses moths to fresh dung so future moth generations fertilize digestible Trichophilus fur algae
