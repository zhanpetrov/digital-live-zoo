Analyze Alligator mississippiensis dry-season refugia, rookery security, and Platalea ajaja mechanoreception. Augustine Rookery, USA to log three ecological targets and answer the research question.

What to look for

  • Alligator dry-season oasis pool — Alligator mississippiensis gator hole. Centre the excavated limestone basin holding dry-season water for marsh fishes
  • Spoonbill tactile sweeping flat — Platalea ajaja Herbst corpuscle foraging. Locate the shallow littoral margin swept by tactile-foraging roseate spoonbills
  • Bald cypress bodyguard rookery — Taxodium distichum nest-protector guild. Scan the cypress canopy where nesting herons, egrets, and spoonbills recruit alligator guards

Source: Nell et al. (2016), PLOS ONE; Kushlan (1974), Ecology — Alligator rookery bodyguard mutualism & tactile spoonbill foraging.

Kids' question: How does a roseate spoonbill catch tiny fish in cloudy swamp water where eyes can't see?

  • Touch sensors inside its spoon-shaped beak snap shut the instant a fish brushes by!

  • It asks frogs for directions

Adults' question: Why do spoonbills, wood storks, and egrets preferentially establish breeding colonies directly above resident alligators?

  • Alligators deter arboreal mesopredators like raccoons and opossums from swimming out to raid nests
  • Alligators incubate bird eggs inside warm mud mounds
  • Cypress trees only grow on alligator backs
  • Birds eat alligator scales for calcium
