What to look for

  • 367 Collins granite eyrie ledge — Falco peregrinus macropus long-term site fidelity. Centre the 34th-floor recessed ledge occupied continuously by peregrines since 1991
  • Sunlit Yarra River & Port Phillip reflection — Malar melanin glare absorption & nictitating sweep. Locate the reflective water corridor where malar stripes preserve retinal contrast
  • Bifoveate spiral-stoop skyway — Logarithmic spiral & 140 Hz flicker fusion. Scan the upper airspace where falcons fly logarithmic spirals preserving straight-head aerodynamics

Source: Vrettos et al. (2021), Biology Letters — Peregrine malar stripes evolve with solar radiation to reduce glare; Tucker (2000), Journal of Experimental Biology — Deep-fovea spiral stoop geometry.

Adults' question: Why do diving peregrine falcons often approach distant prey along a curved logarithmic spiral rather than a straight line (Tucker, 2000)?

  • To keep prey locked on the sharpest angled deep fovea (~40° off-axis) while holding the head straight for minimum aerodynamic drag
  • Because Coriolis forces spin birds counter-clockwise in Victoria
  • Because peregrines have only one functional eye at a time
  • To wind up wing tendons like a rubber-band airplane
Answer

To keep prey locked on the sharpest angled deep fovea (~40° off-axis) while holding the head straight for minimum aerodynamic drag
