About this place

Study Eubalaena australis cyamid callosity photo-ID, mother-calf acoustic crypsis, and Spheniscus–Lama coastal steppe coexistence.

For kids: Explore a wild Patagonian cape where giant whales wear fingerprint bumps, penguins dig desert burrows, and wild llamas watch the waves!

Patagonia Valdés Whales & Steppe · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Right-whale whispering surf bay — Eubalaena australis cyamid callosities. Centre the shallow surf nursery where Eubalaena pairs practice acoustic crypsis
  • Magellanic penguin desert burrows — Spheniscus magellanicus thermal burrows. Locate the coastal shrub steppe excavated by nesting Magellanic penguins
  • Wind-swept guanaco steppe bluff — Lama guanicoe sentinel cliff edge. Scan the arid Patagonian terrace grazed by sentinel Lama guanicoe family groups

Source: Payne et al. (1983) Callosity Photo-ID & Nielsen et al. (2019), J. Exp. Biol. — Acoustic crypsis ('whispering') in Eubalaena australis pairs.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How do scientists recognize hundreds of individual southern right whales returning to Valdés year after year?

  • By each whale's one-of-a-kind pattern of bumpy white head patches!
  • By painting numbers on their tails
  • By counting stripes on their flippers
Answer

By each whale's one-of-a-kind pattern of bumpy white head patches!

Adults' question: Why do nursing southern right whale mothers and calves switch to ultra-low-amplitude 'whisper' calls near Valdés beaches (Nielsen et al., 2019)?

  • Surf-zone noise masks quiet calls within ~200 m so offshore killer whales cannot eavesdrop on calf locations
  • Warm gulf water absorbs all sound frequencies below ten kilohertz
  • Mother whales lose their vocal folds while fasting
  • Quiet calls stun schooling anchovies along pebble beaches
Answer

Surf-zone noise masks quiet calls within ~200 m so offshore killer whales cannot eavesdrop on calf locations
