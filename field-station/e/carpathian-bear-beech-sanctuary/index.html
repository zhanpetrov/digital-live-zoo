About this place

Investigate Carpathian Ursus arctos autumn beech-mast hyperphagia and urea-recycling bone-sparing winter hibernation.

For kids: Roam misty Transylvanian mountain woods where brown bears munch crunchy beechnuts and snooze all winter without losing strength!

What to look for

  • Ancient beech & oak mast slope — Fagus sylvatica autumn hyperphagia. Locate the mature Fagus-Quercus mast stand fueling pre-denning hyperphagia
  • Root-vaulted hillside winter den — Bone-sparing winter hibernation chamber. Centre the north-facing wooded slope suited for thermally stable winter dens
  • Resin-scented pine rub tree — Pedal & dorsal olfactory communication. Scan the ridge wildlife trail featuring traditional scent-rubbing conifers

Source: Tøien et al. (2011), Science & Donahue et al. (2006), J. Experimental Biology — Hibernating Ursus metabolic suppression independent of body temperature and osteoblastic bone preservation.

Field quiz

Kids' question: What amazing superpower keeps a winter-sleeping brown bear strong until spring?

  • Its body recycles nutrients to protect bones and muscles while sleeping on stored autumn fat
  • Squirrels bring hot soup to the den every morning
  • It wakes up every night to lift heavy boulders
Answer

Its body recycles nutrients to protect bones and muscles while sleeping on stored autumn fat

Adults' question: How does Ursus arctos hibernation physiology differ fundamentally from small-mammal deep torpor (e.g., ground squirrels)?

  • Metabolic rate drops ~75% while core temperature stays high (32–34 °C), recycling 100% of urea nitrogen and preserving bone mineral density
  • Core body temperature freezes solid at minus five degrees Celsius
  • Heart rate accelerates to three hundred beats per minute all winter
  • Bears must wake weekly to drink river water and excrete ammonia
Answer

Metabolic rate drops ~75% while core temperature stays high (32–34 °C), recycling 100% of urea nitrogen and preserving bone mineral density
