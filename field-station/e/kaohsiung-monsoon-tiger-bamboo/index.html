What to look for

  • Sun-striped bamboo shadow screen — Dichromatic pheomelanin crypsis. Centre the vertical culm shadows matching disruptive flank striping
  • Cool limestone forest pool — Evaporative & conductive hydro-cooling. Locate the shaded karst pool used by tigers to offload midday heat
  • Curtained banyan & ear-spot trail — White auricular ocelli signaling. Scan the shaded banyan root corridor where auricular ocelli guide cubs

Source: Fennell et al. (2019), Journal of the Royal Society Interface — Dichromatic visual modeling proves orange tiger fur functions as green camouflage to ungulates.

Field quiz

Kids' question: Why doesn't a tiger's bright orange coat give away its hiding spot to sambar deer in green bamboo woods?

  • Deer lack red-sensing eye cones, so orange fur looks leafy green to them!
  • Tigers roll in green moss paint every morning
  • Deer keep their eyes closed while eating bamboo
Answer

Deer lack red-sensing eye cones, so orange fur looks leafy green to them!

Adults' question: According to computational visual modeling by Fennell et al. (2019), why didn't evolutionary selection favor green fur in tigers?

  • Ungulate prey are dichromats that already perceive pheomelanin orange as green, removing selective pressure for novel green pigments
  • Green chlorophyll in fur would attract leaf-cutter ants
  • Tigers hunt exclusively in total pitch-black cave systems
  • Orange fur reflects ultraviolet laser beams that stun prey
Answer

Ungulate prey are dichromats that already perceive pheomelanin orange as green, removing selective pressure for novel green pigments
