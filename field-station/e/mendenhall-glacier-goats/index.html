About this place

Study Oreamnos americanus split-hoof traction biomechanics and primary succession along deglaciated granite cliffs.

For kids: Meet shaggy white climbers whose grippy rubbery hooves cling to tiny ledges high above blue glacier ice!

Mendenhall Ice-Cliff Goats · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Polished granite escape cliffs — Oreamnos americanus refuge terrain. Scan the steep glacially scoured granite walls (>50° slope) used as predator refugia
  • Dense blue glacier ice — Long-wavelength red absorption. Centre the compressed glacial ice zone exhibiting blue spectral scattering
  • Pioneer alder & lupine moraine — Frankia nitrogen-fixing succession. Locate the recently deglaciated moraine colonized by nitrogen-fixing Sitka alder

Source: Lewinson & Stefanyshyn (2016), Zoology — Cloven-hoof compliance & micro-asperity friction in Oreamnos americanus.

Field quiz

Kids' question: What makes a mountain goat's hooves grip slippery rock cliffs so safely?

  • A hard outer edge around a soft, grippy rubber-like pad
  • Sticky tree syrup on their toes
  • Tiny suction tentacles like an octopus
Answer

A hard outer edge around a soft, grippy rubber-like pad

Adults' question: Taxonomically, which lineage does the North American mountain goat (Oreamnos americanus) actually belong to?

  • Rupicaprinae (goat-antelopes allied to chamois, takin, and serows)
  • Domestic Capra hircus feral descendants
  • Cervidae antlered mountain deer
  • Camelidae New World llamas
Answer

Rupicaprinae (goat-antelopes allied to chamois, takin, and serows)
