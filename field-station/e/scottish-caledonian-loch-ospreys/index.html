About this place

Examine Pandion haliaetus reversible zygodactyly, plantar spicules, and trans-Saharan migration to Highland lochs.

For kids: Visit ancient Scottish pine woods where fish-hunting ospreys rotate their outer toes and turn slippery trout nose-first!

What to look for

  • Flat-topped 'Granny' Scots pine — Pinus sylvestris osprey eyrie crown. Locate the open-grown Caledonian Pinus sylvestris crown suited for osprey eyries
  • Peat-stained Highland trout loch — Pandion haliaetus plunge-dive zone. Centre the clear wind-rippled loch surface patrolled by plunging ospreys
  • Heather & blaeberry pinewood floor — Tetrao urogallus understory guild. Scan the Vaccinium-Calluna understory supporting capercaillie and endemic Scottish crossbills

Source: Poole (1989) & Sustaita et al. (2013), Journal of Experimental Biology — Pandion haliaetus reversible zygodactyl grip mechanics and trans-Saharan satellite telemetry.

Field quiz

Kids' question: How do an osprey's special toes help it hang onto a super-slippery fish?

  • One outer toe flips backward so two toes grip each side over spiky foot pads
  • Webbed duck paddles glue the fish to its belly
  • Tiny suction cups like an octopus tentacle
Answer

One outer toe flips backward so two toes grip each side over spiky foot pads

Adults' question: Which combined anatomical specializations distinguish Pandion haliaetus among diurnal raptors?

  • Reversible outer fourth toe, keratinous plantar spicules, and sealable nasal valves
  • Echolocation clicks paired with serrated tooth-like tomia
  • Hollow buoyant leg bones filled with warm helium
  • Complete loss of preen gland oils to sink deeper
Answer

Reversible outer fourth toe, keratinous plantar spicules, and sealable nasal valves
