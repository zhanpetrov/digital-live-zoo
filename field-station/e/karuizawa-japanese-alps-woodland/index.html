About this place

Explore compositional vocal syntax in Parus minor (Suzuki et al., 2016) and Sciurus lis scatter-hoarding seed dispersal.

For kids: Listen to clever Japanese tits combine bird-words into real sentences while tufted squirrels plant future walnut trees!

Karuizawa Talking-Bird Woods · 360° Habitat Camp

What to look for

  • Mixed-flock songbird boughs — Parus minor ABC-D syntax perch. Locate the mid-canopy perches where Parus minor broadcasts ABC-D calls
  • Walnut-hoarding mossy forest floor — Sciurus lis Juglans scatter-hoarding. Centre the volcanic-ash forest floor used for spatial scatter-hoarding
  • Japanese larch volcanic slope — Larix kaempferi pumice succession. Scan the deciduous Japanese larch stand colonizing Mount Asama pumice

Source: Suzuki, Wheatcroft & Griesser (2016), Nature Communications — Experimental evidence for compositional syntax in bird calls (Parus minor, Karuizawa).

Ask together: In human language, 'dog bites man' means something very different from 'man bites dog'—why does word order matter to tiny forest birds too?

Field quiz

Kids' question: What happens if scientists play the Japanese tit's 'Watch out—come here!' sentence backwards?

  • They ignore it because the backwards word order breaks their bird grammar
  • They fly backwards through the trees
  • They fall fast asleep
Answer

They ignore it because the backwards word order breaks their bird grammar

Adults' question: What did Suzuki et al. (2016) demonstrate in Karuizawa woodland regarding Parus minor vocalizations?

  • Rule-governed compositional syntax previously thought unique to human language
  • Ultrasonic echolocation inside hollow walnut trunks
  • Magnetoreceptive duet singing synchronized to volcanic tremors
  • Vocal mimicry of human bullet-train chimes
Answer

Rule-governed compositional syntax previously thought unique to human language
